Job Market Paper

Informational Lobbying for Tariff Exclusions: A Microfounded Model
This paper develops a signaling model of firm lobbying for tariff exclusions under Section 301 of the U.S.–China trade war. Firms hold private information about their reliance on imports and strategically lobby to influence government decisions. The model introduces an alignment condition linking political weights and economic primitives, and is calibrated using sectoral data to quantify the net value of the informational lobbying channel.

Additional Research

Lobbying Influence on EXIM Bank Decisions in the United States
This paper investigates the role of lobbying by exporters and domestic competitors in shaping the approval decisions of the U.S. Export–Import Bank. A novel dataset links EXIM authorizations to detailed lobbying activity, and a structural model captures the equilibrium effects of competing signals in a politically-weighted objective function.
